Where we start

The assessment covered 67 people: 6 sales leaders, 15 sales managers, and 46 salespeople across Fleet, Commercial, the company-owned stores, and Extendobed. Three findings shape the plan.

  • The people are ready. 93% of the salespeople and 87% of the managers are coachable, desire and outlook are high, and the team is strong at building relationships and presenting. The gap is skill and structure, not willingness.
  • “Beat on price” is a selling-value problem. Most salespeople accept comparison shopping and think-it-overs, avoid the money conversation, and present rather than uncover what matters to the buyer. When the buyer’s reasons are never uncovered, price is the only thing left to compare.
  • There is no structure to hold it together. Managers spend about a third of their time managing salespeople. Coaching happens on request. There is no common sales process, no working pipeline, and few personal goals or plans to hold anyone accountable to.

The order of work follows the triangle we drew in discovery: strategy, then structure, then staff, then skills. Adrian sets the strategy on November 10. Lushin helps build the structure under it, then the skills on top of it.

How we would work

  • Leaders first. Managers and leaders learn the process, pipeline, coaching, and accountability before the salespeople do, so every session with the sales team is reinforced by a manager who already owns it.
  • One set of fundamentals, applied by group. Sales process, pipeline milestones, qualifying, and selling value are the same concepts for everyone. How they are applied is built with each group, so Fleet, Commercial, and Retail each get a process that fits how they go to market, including the pickup and integrated solution conversation on the commercial side.
  • Structure and reps, not an event. This group absorbs by repetition. Sessions are every two weeks during the leadership rollout, then monthly. Coaching is weekly inside Adrian, and nothing moves on until the group has it.

What’s in place by March

We’ll measure the leadership phase by what’s built and being used. By the end of February:

  • Fleet, Commercial, and Retail each have a written sales process, built on the ideal milestones for that group and designed to fix the spots where deals get stuck.
  • The pipeline has clear stages, and a deal moves only when it meets the next stage’s criteria.
  • Every manager reviews one or two key numbers weekly, and the forecast comes from those numbers.
  • Every manager’s team has a plan and a number, and so does every salesperson on it.
  • Managers hold weekly one-on-ones and can train, coach, mentor, and hold people accountable.

So when the salespeople start in March, their managers are already running all of it, and what the salespeople learn gets reinforced every week.

December through February: leadership rollout

  • All 15 managers with their leaders, every two weeks, virtual.
  • Sales process and pipeline for each group, then coaching and accountability: training, coaching, mentoring, and holding weekly one-on-ones.
  • Each manager leaves with a plan for their team and the numbers they are aiming at.
  • Monthly check-in with Marc on where the plan stands.

March: sales team rollout

  • All 46 salespeople with their managers, in person.
  • Managers are already fluent, so they reinforce what the sales team is learning from day one.

March through August

  • Sales team, monthly: getting to the right person, uncovering compelling reasons, setting expectations up front, and the money conversation.
  • Leaders and managers, monthly: coaching to the process and managing the pipeline.
  • Marc, monthly: where the plan stands, share key initiatives and company goals to influence training focus.
  • In person every four to six months: sales team and leadership together.

September onward

  • Sales team sessions are split by division, so the work gets more specific to each market.
  • Leaders and managers continue monthly, with coaching and accountability by division.
  • At the twelve-month mark, everyone is reassessed to show where they started and where they are now (Lushin covers this).
